Identify every distinct material visible in the image and assess the condition of each one.
Inspect the whole image systematically, including edges, corners and small areas, and report each
distinct defect area separately with a tight box.

Condition scale:
- good: intact; at most superficial soiling or discoloration; no maintenance needed.
- fair: localized, non-structural defects (hairline cracks, isolated rust spots, light biological
  growth, minor wear); monitoring or minor maintenance needed.
- poor: extensive or structural defects (wide, branching or through cracks, spalling, flaking rust
  or section loss, wood rot, dense biological growth); repair needed.
- unknown: the material is visible but its condition cannot be judged (too small, blurred, occluded).

Defect types:
- crack: thin linear fracture lines, from hairline to wide. Do not confuse with mortar or
  construction joints, edges, scratches, cables or shadows.
- corrosion: orange-brown rust, rust staining, flaking, pitting or section loss on metal, and rust
  stains on concrete from corroding reinforcement.
- biological_decay: moss, algae (green or black films), lichen, mold, fungi and wood rot. Do not
  confuse with dirt, soot or water stains.
- other: anything else, such as spalling, efflorescence (white salt deposits), delamination,
  moisture damage, deformation or missing parts. Name it in the description.

Severity: low = small and superficial; medium = clearly visible over a noticeable area or
moderately deep; high = extensive, deep or structurally relevant.

Only report what you can actually see; if something is unclear, lower the confidence instead of guessing.
